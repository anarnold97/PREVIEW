## 1. Timing: When Do We Decide?

**This decision does not need to be made today.**

We are currently in the **pilot phase** (pro-bono, open-source). We have no revenue, no contracts, and no liability exposure. Therefore:

*   **No action is required now.** We can operate as individuals for the pilot.
*   **The trigger point** is the moment we sign our first **paid** client contract or invoice.
*   **The goal** is to have the structure decided *before* that first invoice is sent, not before we start talking to clients.

This paper exists so that when we *are* ready to go live, we don't have to panic about the legal setup. We can make a calm, informed choice.

---

## 2. Why This Matters Later

When we transition to paid work, we face a cross-border reality (**US** for MFH, **Ireland** for AA). The wrong choice could create:
*   **Tax Nexus Risks:** Accidentally triggering tax obligations in both countries.
*   **Personal Liability:** Exposing personal assets if a client sues for negligence.
*   **Client Friction:** Enterprise clients often refuse to sign contracts with individuals.

The purpose of this paper is to map the options so we can choose a structure that protects us without drowning in admin.

---

## 3. The Options

### Option A: Remain Unincorporated (Sole Proprietorship / Sole Trader)

Each partner invoices individually from their own country. No formal business registration.

**How it works:**
*   **MFH:** Invoices as an individual US sole proprietor (possibly using an EIN).
*   **AA:** Invoices as an Irish sole trader (registered with Revenue).
*   **Contracting:** Contracts are signed personally by each of us, or we split the work into two separate agreements.

**Advantages:**
*   **Zero setup cost.** No filing fees, no annual returns.
*   **Immediate.** Can invoice tomorrow if needed.
*   **Simple taxes.** Income declared on personal returns (Schedule C in US; Form 11 in IE).

**Disadvantages:**
*   **Unlimited Personal Liability.** If a client sues, your *personal* assets (home, savings) are at risk. There is no corporate shield.
*   **Cross-Border Mess.** A US client hiring an Irish sole trader creates "permanent establishment" risks. Clients may withhold tax (backup withholding) because they don't know your status.
*   **Split Identity.** It looks like two freelancers, not one consultancy. Harder to sell a unified "practice."
*   **IP Ownership.** Without a company, IP ownership is a messy joint ownership that could cause issues later.
*   **Client Friction.** Many enterprise clients will not sign a contract with an individual due to liability concerns.

**Suited to:** Pro-bono pilots, very small low-risk gigs, or the very first few paid contracts if liability is negligible.

---

### Option B: Form a Single Limited Liability Company (LLC or Ltd)

We form **one** legal entity in one jurisdiction, and the other partner becomes a member/contractor.

**Scenario 1: US LLC (Delaware/Wyoming/Nevada)**
*   **Structure:** MFH is the Manager/Member. AA is a non-resident member/contractor.
*   **Pros:** Strong liability shield. US clients love LLCs. Easy to open a US business bank account.
*   **Cons:** AA (in Ireland) may face complex US tax filing requirements (Form 5472/1120-F) even with $0 profit if not structured perfectly. Irish tax residency rules still apply to AA's income.

**Scenario 2: Irish Limited Company (Ltd)**
*   **Structure:** AA is the Director/Shareholder. MFH is a non-resident shareholder/contractor.
*   **Pros:** Simple for AA. EU clients may prefer an EU entity.
*   **Cons:** US clients may be wary of contracting with an Irish Ltd (tax withholding issues). MFH may face US tax complications receiving dividends from a foreign corp.

**Advantages (General):**
*   **Limited Liability.** Personal assets are protected.
*   **Unified Brand.** One entity, one bank account, one contract.
*   **IP Clarity.** The company owns the IP, not the individuals.

**Disadvantages (General):**
*   **Admin Burden.** Annual filings, accounting fees, potential double-tax treaty complexity.
*   **Cost.** Setup fees + ongoing compliance costs in the chosen jurisdiction.
*   **Cross-Border Tax Complexity.** We will likely need a specialist accountant to handle the US/IE treaty to avoid double taxation.

**Suited to:** First paid engagement, especially with enterprise clients who require a corporate counterparty.

---

### Option C: The "Virtual" Partnership (Contractual Alliance)

We remain unincorporated but sign a **Master Services Agreement (MSA)** between ourselves that defines:
*   Joint ownership of IP.
*   Indemnity clauses (we indemnify each other).
*   How we split revenue and expenses.
*   Liability caps.

**Pros:** Cheap, flexible, no corporate admin.
**Cons:** Does **not** provide a liability shield against third-party lawsuits. Clients may still refuse to sign.

---

## 4. Key Considerations for Decision

1.  **Liability Appetite:** Are we comfortable risking personal assets for the first few projects? (If no -> Option B).
2.  **Client Profile:** Are our first targets US enterprises (prefer US LLC), EU firms (prefer EU Ltd), or Open Source (don't care)?
3.  **Admin Tolerance:** Are we willing to pay an accountant ~€1k/$1k/year to handle cross-border filings?
4.  **Tax Treaty:** The US-Ireland tax treaty exists, but navigating it requires professional advice.
